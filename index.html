How it works

It watches. You decide. Then it gets to know your shop.

Seekur turns the cameras you already have into a second pair of eyes. Here is what happens, from the day you install it.

01 · SET UP

Four short steps to start

Make an account, let Seekur look at your computer, add a camera (or try the demo video first), and connect your phone. Seekur tells you how many cameras your computer can watch.

02 · WATCH

It watches your cameras

Seekur runs on a computer in your shop and looks at the video from each camera, all day. The video stays on that computer.

03 · SPOT

It notices someone hiding something

It follows how people move and where their hands go. When a hand takes an item and then goes into a bag or clothing in a way that looks like hiding it, Seekur raises a flag.

04 · ALERT

You get a message with a picture

A snapshot with the person framed lands on your phone, with two buttons: Confirm theft and Dismiss. The short video clip waits in the app. Nobody is accused automatically.

05 · ACT

You decide

Confirm, and the alarm sounds in the shop. Dismiss, and nothing happens, except that Seekur now knows that was a false alarm.

06 · LEARN

Each camera gets to know its own view

Every Confirm and Dismiss teaches that one camera what is normal at that spot, so it can stop raising the same false alarm. You can also teach it yourself with your own clips.

What Seekur looks at

  • How people move, and where their hands go
  • Bags, and items people pick up
  • With the Plus plan: anyone in a restricted area you draw, people in the shop after closing time, and someone lying on the floor

What Seekur never does

  • Recognise faces, or work out who anyone is
  • Send your camera video anywhere
  • Sound the alarm on its own: a person always confirms first
  • Record your cameras around the clock: your own recorder does that

It looks after itself, and tells you when something goes wrong

Checking a camera takes real computing power, so Seekur measures what your computer can do while it runs. It watches as many cameras as the computer can manage at full speed, in the order you choose, and keeps showing the rest live. If your computer has a graphics chip that would help, Seekur tests it first and only uses it if it gives the same answers and is clearly faster. If anything stops working, Seekur quietly goes back to the processor and tells you.

It also keeps the computer from falling asleep while it watches, and sends a message to your phone when a camera goes offline, is covered, freezes or is moved. And if the whole computer stops reporting, our server messages you too, so a silent failure doesn't go unnoticed.

How we measured it

  • 177 video clips of staged shoplifting and normal shopping, from a public research dataset.
  • Every clip was judged by a model that had never seen it (5-fold cross-validation), so memorising can't flatter the numbers.
  • Measured through the same detection code the store runs, safety checks included.

What it means for you

  • Treat alerts as a second pair of eyes, not a guarantee.
  • The clips were filmed in a room set up like a shop. Real stores are harder: crowds, shelves in the way, ceiling cameras.
  • These figures are for the built-in model, before it learns anything from your cameras. Your own store will differ.

Gets better over time

It learns your store, camera by camera.

Taking a phone off a table and pocketing it is theft in a phone shop and an ordinary moment in a café. So Seekur doesn't mix stores: every camera learns only from what you confirm or dismiss on that camera, on your own computer.

On your computer · nothing is shared with other stores

01

You decide

Confirm or dismiss an alert as usual. That's the only thing you do.

02

The computer keeps the clip

Seekur doesn't record everything: your recorder does that. It keeps only the clip of each alert and your answer, for 1, 3 or 7 days, as you choose. What the camera learns from your answers is kept as a small record of body points, with no video, for up to 90 days.

03

The camera tries to improve

About once a day, a camera with enough decisions (around 16) trains a new version of itself in the background.

04

It has to win to stay

The new version is tested on decisions it never trained on. It replaces the old one, silently and without a restart, only if it does better and catches no fewer thefts.

A new camera next to an old one

Start it from a copy of what the old camera learned, then it keeps learning its own view. Seekur checks that the copy fits, and goes back to the built-in start if it doesn't.

Removed a camera?

What it learned is kept for 90 days, so a replacement camera can take it over. After that it is deleted.

Not happy with it?

Press "Start over" on any camera to wipe what it learned and begin again.

Nothing to hide

Settings shows what each camera has learned, in plain words, and what it is still waiting for.

Or teach it faster yourself.

Add your own clips from a camera and tag each one as theft or normal. Show it the staff putting a phone in a pocket, or a customer taking their own keys, and tell it those are fine. Seekur reads the body movement in each clip, keeps only that (never the video), and starts learning straight away instead of waiting for the daily check.

Privacy

Smart, without being creepy.

Video stays in your store

Live video is analysed on your own computer and never uploaded. Seekur keeps only the clips of alerts, for as long as you choose, and learns from them there. What it learns is kept as a small record of body points, with no video, for up to 90 days. Passwords are stored encrypted. Alert snapshots go to the phone apps you connect, and you can switch the picture off for any of them.

No face recognition

Seekur reads body movement and bags, never faces. It doesn't know who anyone is, and doesn't try.

A person decides

Every alert is a "please check this". Nothing is escalated and no alarm sounds until someone confirms.

How we got these numbers. On a 2018 laptop (4 cores, 8 threads) Seekur completed 5 to 7 theft checks a second in total, depending on what else the computer was doing. One camera needs 5, and Seekur keeps 15% spare, so that laptop sometimes counts as one camera and sometimes as none. Each extra camera needs roughly three more cores of the same kind. We have only measured one computer so far, and none of the graphics options yet on real graphics hardware, so we make no promise about how much faster they are. Treat the table as a guide: the speed test Seekur runs on your computer, for the processor and for each graphics option, is the number to trust.

Questions

Good to know.

What does Seekur actually do?

It watches your shop cameras for someone hiding an item in a bag or clothing. When it sees something that looks like it, it sends a picture to your phone with two buttons: Confirm theft or Dismiss. Confirm, and an alarm sounds in the shop. It doesn't stop anyone by itself: it tells you quickly so you can.

Will it catch every shoplifter?

No camera system does. On staged footage, the built-in version caught about half of the thefts, and 93% of its alerts were real. Your cameras will differ, and Seekur can only learn from alerts it raises, so a theft it misses teaches it nothing unless you add that clip yourself. Think of it as a second pair of eyes.

What happens when it gets it wrong?

If it raises a false alarm, press Dismiss. The camera remembers it, and with enough of these it learns to stop raising that kind of alarm. If you notice it missed something, you can add the clip yourself and tag it as theft.

Do I need to be technical to set it up?

No. Seekur walks you through four short steps: your account, your computer, a camera, and your phone. The only technical part is the camera's video address, which is in the camera's manual or app, and Seekur explains where to find it. You can try the built-in demo video first, with no camera at all.

Do I need new cameras?

Usually not. Any network camera that can share its video works, and almost all of them can. Seekur doesn't replace your recorder: it sits next to it.

Do I need to install a Seekur app on my phone?

No. Alerts come through free apps you may already have, like ntfy or Telegram, or by email. Seekur shows you how to connect each one, step by step.

Does video leave my shop?

No. Live video is analysed on your computer. Seekur's servers only handle your account, your subscription and the phone Confirm and Dismiss buttons. Alert snapshots go to the phone apps you connect. Seekur keeps the clips of alerts on your computer for 1, 3 or 7 days, as you choose, and a small record of body movement (no video, no images) for up to 90 days so each camera can keep learning.

Does it recognise faces?

No. Seekur reads body movement and bags. It doesn't know who anyone is and doesn't try to.

How does Seekur learn?

Each camera learns from your Confirm and Dismiss decisions on that camera, on your computer, so a phone shop and a café don't confuse each other. Once a camera has about 16 decisions, it tries a new version of itself roughly once a day. It switches only if the new version does better on decisions it hasn't practised on, and without missing more thefts. The switch happens quietly in the background.

Can I teach it myself?

Yes. In Settings, pick a camera and add video clips from it, tagged as theft or as normal. For example, show it staff putting a phone in a pocket and tag that normal. Seekur reads the body movement in each clip, keeps only that (never the video), and starts learning straight away.

I added a camera next to one that already learned. Does it start from zero?

Not unless you want it to. When you add a camera, you can start it from a copy of what another camera learned. It then keeps learning its own view, and Seekur checks the copy actually suits the new view before trusting it.

What happens to what a camera learned if I remove it?

It is kept for 90 days. If you add the camera back, or a replacement, you can choose to take over what it learned. After 90 days it is deleted. You can also delete it sooner in Settings.

Does it work without internet?

Watching and detection run on your computer, so they keep working if the internet drops for a while. Phone alerts do need the internet to reach your phone, and Seekur checks in with its servers now and then about your subscription.

What computer do I need?

See the specs table. The first time you open Seekur, it checks your processor, memory and graphics chip, tests which of them can run the detection and how fast, picks the fastest one that passes, and tells you how many cameras your computer can watch. You can change the choice in Settings.

How many cameras can my computer handle?

Checking a camera takes real computing power, five times a second per camera. Seekur measures how fast your computer is while it runs and tells you how many cameras it can watch. It watches that many, in the order you choose, and keeps showing the rest live. If the computer gets busier or freer, it adjusts by itself. When you subscribe, Seekur suggests paying only for the cameras your computer can actually watch.

Can I use the same computer for other things?

Yes, but detection uses a lot of the processor, so a computer that does little else works best. If it gets busy, Seekur notices and watches fewer cameras for a while instead of falling behind.

Does it use my graphics card?

It can. On Windows and Mac, Seekur tests the graphics chip the first time and uses it only if it is clearly faster and gives the same answers. On Linux with an NVIDIA graphics card, it can download some extra files (about 2 GB, with your agreement) to use it. If it ever stops working, Seekur goes back to the processor on its own.

What if a camera or the computer goes offline?

Seekur reconnects to a camera by itself after a drop, and says in the app when one is offline. If the computer restarts, Seekur can start again by itself when you sign in.

What is the difference between Core and Plus?

Core is theft detection with phone alerts and the alarm. Plus adds three extras: anyone in a restricted area you draw on the camera picture, anyone in the shop after closing time, and someone lying on the floor. Core is $6 per camera a month, and the Plus extras add $3 on each camera that uses them ($9 in all). You can mix them: Plus on the stock-room camera, Core on the rest.

Does Seekur know who is staff?

No. Seekur doesn't recognise people, so a restricted area alerts for anyone who stays in it, staff included. That is why each area can be set to be on all the time or only while your shop is closed, and why you can pause an area for 15 minutes while someone works there. Areas work best where nobody should be: a back exit, a stock room after closing, a fenced-off corner.

Can I pay for only some of my cameras?

Yes. Each camera has two switches in Seekur: Watch for theft, and Plus detections. You pay only for the cameras with theft detection on, and the Plus extra only for those that use it. A camera with both switched off still shows live.

I upgraded my computer in the middle of the month. What do I pay?

Switch on theft detection for the extra camera and press Update my plan in Settings. Seekur shows the price before you confirm: it is only the difference for the rest of the current month, not a full month. The new camera starts being watched straight away.

What if my computer goes to sleep, turns off or loses the internet?

Seekur keeps the computer awake while it watches. If the computer stops reporting anyway, our server tells you by Telegram, ntfy or email, and tells you again when it's back. A camera that goes offline, is covered or freezes also gets a message. Detection itself runs on your computer, so a short internet outage doesn't stop it, but phone alerts wait for the connection.

Can I try it first without alerts going to my phone?

Yes. Practice mode saves alerts in the app but sends no phone message and no alarm. Look through them, confirm or dismiss, and the cameras learn. Turn it off when you trust it.

Can different people get different alerts?

Yes. For each phone app you connect, you choose which kinds of alert it gets, when (always, only while the shop is open, or only while it is closed), and whether it includes the picture. For example: you get everything, the manager only gets after-hours alerts, and staff get theft alerts while the shop is open.

I set it up on one computer. Can I use my account on another one?

Yes. On the new computer, choose "I already have an account: sign in" and use your email and password. Your plan comes with it and no new trial starts. If a computer is replaced or lost, you can sign it out of your account in Settings. Several computers can share one subscription: together they can't watch more cameras than you pay for.

I forgot my password.

On the sign-in screen choose "Forgot password?". Seekur emails a 6-digit code to the address on the account; enter it with a new password and you are signed in. You can also sign in with an emailed code instead of the password, and change the password any time in Settings.

What if I sign up with an email that already has an account?

Seekur tells you the account exists and offers to sign you in instead. Nobody can add a store to an account just by typing its email address: signing in needs the password (or a code sent to that address).

I have more than one store. Do I need more than one account?

No. One account can hold any number of stores. Each store has its own cameras and its own 14-day trial, and the whole account is billed on one invoice. Settings shows the total and what each store contributes, line by line. Add a store from Settings, and use it on this computer or on another one.

Does what my cameras learned follow my account to another computer?

Learning stays on the computer where it happened: Seekur never uploads it. To move it, save a camera's learning to a file in Settings, copy the file over (a USB stick works) and import it on the other computer. You can import several files at once, and each one becomes a starting point when you add a camera there. The file holds the learned model and body points only, never video.

Do I need to know my camera's address?

Not always. "Find cameras" looks for cameras on your network and tries the usual video addresses for their brand with the username and password you give it. If yours doesn't announce itself, the camera's manual or app has the address, and Seekur explains where to look.

How do I get updates and help?

Seekur tells you in the app when a new version is out. If something is wrong, Settings has a button that saves a support file with the computer's details and Seekur's log, with no video and no passwords, that you can send to us.

Is my camera password safe?

Camera passwords, email passwords and similar secrets are stored encrypted, with the key kept in your operating system's own key store. They are never sent to Seekur's server.

Is Seekur available in my language?

Not yet: the app and website are in English for now.

Is there a free trial?

Yes. Every new shop gets 14 days of Plus, with everything switched on. If you subscribe before the 14 days are up, your plan starts straight away and the trial ends then. After the trial, live view keeps working, and detection needs Core ($6 per camera a month) or Plus ($9).
